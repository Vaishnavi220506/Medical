## What brain tumors are
A brain tumor is a mass of abnormal cells in the brain. Primary brain tumors start in the brain itself; secondary (metastatic) tumors spread to the brain from cancers elsewhere, such as lung, breast, skin (melanoma) or kidney. Metastases are more common in adults than primary brain tumors. Tumors are graded by the WHO classification of CNS tumors from grade 1 (slow-growing) to grade 4 (fast-growing and aggressive). The 2021 WHO classification combines microscopic appearance with molecular markers such as IDH mutation and 1p/19q codeletion.

## Common symptoms
Symptoms depend on tumor size and location and can include headaches (often worse in the morning), seizures, nausea or vomiting, changes in vision, speech or personality, weakness or numbness on one side of the body, balance problems and memory difficulties. These symptoms have many other causes too.

## How brain tumors are diagnosed
MRI with and without gadolinium contrast is the main imaging test. A definitive diagnosis and grade usually require tissue from a biopsy or surgery, examined by a neuropathologist, including molecular testing. Imaging alone cannot establish the exact tumor type or grade.

## Treatment overview
Treatment is planned by a multidisciplinary team and depends on tumor type, grade, molecular profile, location and the patient's overall health. Options include surgery (maximal safe resection), radiation therapy, chemotherapy (for example temozolomide for glioblastoma), targeted therapy, tumor treating fields, and active surveillance for some slow-growing tumors. Steroids such as dexamethasone are often used to reduce swelling (edema).
