## RANO for gliomas
RANO measures enhancing lesions by the product of the longest diameter and the longest perpendicular diameter on the same slice. Compared with the baseline or best response: a decrease of 50% or more is a partial response, disappearance of all enhancing disease is a complete response, an increase of 25% or more is progressive disease, and anything in between is stable disease. Full RANO also requires stable or decreasing steroid dose, assessment of non-enhancing (T2/FLAIR) disease, absence of new lesions and clinical status. The updated RANO 2.0 criteria (2023) refine these rules.

## RECIST 1.1 for solid tumors
RECIST 1.1 uses the sum of the longest diameters of target lesions. A decrease of at least 30% is partial response, an increase of at least 20% (and at least 5 mm absolute) is progressive disease, disappearance is complete response, and otherwise the disease is stable.

## Pseudoprogression
After radiotherapy with temozolomide, enhancement can temporarily increase because of treatment effects rather than tumor growth (pseudoprogression), most often within 12 weeks. Apparent growth soon after treatment therefore needs careful interpretation by specialists.
