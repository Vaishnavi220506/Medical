## MRI sequences
T1-weighted images show anatomy well; fat is bright and fluid is dark. T1 post-contrast (T1ce, T1Gd) images are taken after gadolinium injection; areas where the blood-brain barrier is broken, such as active tumor, become bright ("enhance"). T2-weighted images make fluid and most abnormalities bright. FLAIR (fluid-attenuated inversion recovery) is T2-weighted with the signal of normal cerebrospinal fluid suppressed, which makes edema and infiltrative tumor stand out.

## Common terms
Enhancement: brightening after contrast. Edema: swelling from fluid around a tumor. Necrosis: dead tissue, often a dark center inside an enhancing rim. Mass effect: pressure of a mass on nearby structures. Midline shift: displacement of brain structures across the midline, a sign of significant mass effect. Hyperintense / hypointense: brighter / darker than surrounding tissue.

## Radiological convention
Axial images are usually displayed as if looking up from the patient's feet, so the patient's right side appears on the left of the image.
