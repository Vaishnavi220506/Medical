## What a glioma is
Gliomas are tumors that arise from glial cells, the supporting cells of the brain and spinal cord. Main types are astrocytoma, oligodendroglioma and ependymoma. Glioblastoma (IDH-wildtype, WHO grade 4) is the most common and most aggressive primary malignant brain tumor in adults.

## Glioma on MRI
High-grade gliomas typically show an irregular enhancing rim on T1 post-contrast (T1ce) images surrounding a dark necrotic core, with surrounding hyperintense signal on T2/FLAIR that represents edema and infiltrating tumor. Low-grade gliomas are often non-enhancing and appear as T2/FLAIR hyperintense areas with less mass effect. Enhancement alone is not a perfect indicator of grade.

## Tumor sub-regions used in research (BraTS)
The BraTS challenge labels three sub-regions: the necrotic / non-enhancing tumor core, the peritumoral edema (FLAIR hyperintensity), and the enhancing tumor. Combined, they form the "whole tumor"; the enhancing tumor plus necrotic core form the "tumor core". These definitions are what OncoVision's brain segmenter is trained to reproduce.

## Outlook
Prognosis varies widely with type, grade and molecular features. Low-grade IDH-mutant gliomas may be managed for many years, while glioblastoma has a median survival of around 15 months with standard treatment (surgery followed by radiotherapy with temozolomide).
