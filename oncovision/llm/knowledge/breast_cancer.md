## Breast cancer
Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed cancer in women worldwide. Early detection through screening and prompt evaluation of breast symptoms greatly improves outcomes. Most breast lumps are not cancer.

## Breast ultrasound
Ultrasound is used to evaluate lumps, dense breasts and findings on mammography, and to guide biopsies. Features that suggest a benign lesion include an oval shape, circumscribed margins, orientation parallel to the skin and uniform appearance (for example fibroadenomas). Features that raise suspicion of malignancy include irregular shape, non-circumscribed (angular, microlobulated or spiculated) margins, a "taller-than-wide" orientation and posterior acoustic shadowing. Simple cysts are fluid-filled, anechoic (black) and benign.

## Breast MRI
Contrast-enhanced (DCE) breast MRI is the most sensitive breast imaging test. It is used for screening women at high risk, measuring the extent of a known cancer before surgery, checking the other breast, and following how a tumor responds to chemotherapy given before surgery (neoadjuvant therapy). Images are taken before and several times after a gadolinium contrast injection; cancers usually take up contrast quickly. Size on MRI is often reported as the longest diameter, and response to treatment can be followed with RECIST-style measurements. MRI also shows many benign enhancing findings, so suspicious areas need a biopsy.

## BI-RADS categories
The ACR BI-RADS system standardizes reports: 0 incomplete (needs more imaging), 1 negative, 2 benign, 3 probably benign (short-interval follow-up, under 2% malignancy risk), 4 suspicious (biopsy recommended; 4A, 4B, 4C with increasing risk), 5 highly suggestive of malignancy (95% or more), 6 known biopsy-proven malignancy. Only a radiologist can assign a BI-RADS category; a biopsy is needed to confirm cancer.

## Treatment overview
Treatment can include surgery (lumpectomy or mastectomy), radiation therapy, chemotherapy, hormone therapy for hormone-receptor-positive cancers, and targeted therapy such as HER2-directed drugs, chosen according to stage and tumor biology.
