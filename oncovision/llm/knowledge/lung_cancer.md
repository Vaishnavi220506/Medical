## Lung cancer
Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer death worldwide. Smoking is the main risk factor; others include second-hand smoke, radon, air pollution and occupational exposures. The main types are non-small cell lung cancer (about 85%, including adenocarcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma) and small cell lung cancer.

## Lung nodules on CT
A lung nodule is a rounded opacity up to 30 mm; larger lesions are called masses. Most small nodules are benign (old infection scars, granulomas). Features that increase concern include larger size, growth over time, irregular or spiculated margins, and part-solid composition. Low-dose CT screening is recommended for certain high-risk people with a heavy smoking history.

## Lung-RADS
The ACR Lung-RADS system categorizes screening CT findings from 1 (negative) and 2 (benign appearance) to 3 (probably benign, 6-month follow-up) and 4A, 4B, 4X (suspicious, needing further work-up such as PET-CT or biopsy). Size thresholds depend on whether the nodule is solid, part-solid or ground-glass. For a solid nodule on a first (baseline) screening CT, size is the mean of the long and short axis: under 6 mm is category 2 (continue yearly screening), 6 to under 8 mm is category 3 (low-dose CT in 6 months), 8 to under 15 mm is category 4A (low-dose CT in 3 months; PET-CT may be considered), and 15 mm or more is category 4B (diagnostic chest CT, PET-CT and/or tissue sampling). Growth on later scans, part-solid or ground-glass nodules and suspicious features change the category, so only a radiologist can assign it.

## Nodule volume
Nodule size can also be measured as volume in cubic millimetres, which detects growth more reliably than diameter. Volume doubling time is used in some screening programmes to judge whether a nodule is growing like a cancer.

## Treatment overview
Depending on stage and type, treatment may include surgery, radiation therapy (including stereotactic body radiotherapy for early tumors), chemotherapy, targeted therapy for tumors with driver mutations (such as EGFR or ALK) and immunotherapy.
