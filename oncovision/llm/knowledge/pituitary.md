## What a pituitary tumor is
Most pituitary tumors are pituitary adenomas (now also called pituitary neuroendocrine tumors, PitNETs). They are almost always benign. They grow in the pituitary gland at the base of the skull, in a bony pocket called the sella turcica. Tumors under 10 mm are microadenomas; 10 mm or larger are macroadenomas.

## Symptoms
Some adenomas produce excess hormones (for example prolactin, growth hormone or ACTH), causing conditions such as infertility, acromegaly or Cushing's disease. Larger tumors can press on the optic chiasm and cause loss of peripheral vision, or cause headaches and low hormone levels.

## Pituitary tumors on MRI
Dedicated pituitary MRI with thin slices and contrast is used. Macroadenomas may extend upward and show a "snowman" or "figure-of-eight" shape. Evaluation also includes blood hormone tests and vision field testing.

## Treatment
Options include monitoring, medication (for example dopamine agonists such as cabergoline for prolactinomas), transsphenoidal surgery (through the nose) and radiation therapy.
